Direct Weight Engineering Beats Gradient Descent Only When Structure Is Verifiable and Aligned

Research series by Niklas Sikorra (drniklas.com) · Repository: upscale_net (01–06) · All metrics extracted directly from logged JSON/eval artifacts
Key Takeaway

Writing structure directly into neural weights works—and achieves 100% out-of-distribution generalization at 8–16× training length—whenever an exact verifier or calibrated subspace separates signal from interference. Where that alignment is missing, raw parameter growth buys a rounding error, unverified self-training stalls, and sequential weight edits collide nonlinearly in downstream layers.

Situation

Neural networks normally acquire skills only via massive gradient descent over trillions of tokens, leaving weights opaque and expensive to expand or update.

Complication

Naively adding parameters, self-training without exact feedback, or stacking LoRA updates sequentially either underperforms simple controls or erases prior knowledge.

Question

Across growth, weight synthesis, neural grafting, continual distillation, and recurrent priors, where does direct weight structure genuinely beat standard SGD?

Answer

It wins sharply on exact algorithmic compilation & calibrated grafting (03, 04), buys 4.3× retention via orthogonal slots (05), and hits clear scaling walls everywhere else (01, 02, 06).

Experiment 04 · 04-neural-graft/robust_graft_exp.py · post.md

Neural Graft: A Written Circuit Stays Exact at 16× Training Length Inside Frozen Qwen2.5-1.5B

100% on Every Seed & Test · Tuned LoRA Near Chance

Bottom line: A small causal Transformer whose weights are written directly, not trained, is wired into frozen Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct: a read-in fitted in closed form on Qwen's embeddings (no task labels, clamped to exact 0/1) and a zero-initialized write-out of 3,072–15,360 numbers, the only trained part, fitted on lengths 4–8 only. On three real-token tasks (column addition, balanced brackets, ascending digits) the written circuit scores 100% on every seed (5) and every test, including 64-digit carry chains and a hard bracket set at length 128 (16×). There, tuned LoRA (18.5M params, 3 seeds) scores 52.3% and 53.6%, and a trained circuit of the same shape (5 seeds) 48.3% and 50.1%—near chance. All arms see the same 512 test cases per length; baselines are tuned on in-range validation data. An earlier single-seed run on synthetic tokens is superseded by these results.

Carry chain: every column between the lowest and the top sums to 9, so one carry crosses the whole number (on random 64-digit numbers LoRA scores 99.8%, but a rule using the top two columns already gets 94.8%). Hard brackets: wrong strings have equal counts, start with "(" and end with ")". Brackets "in range" = hard set at 4–8.
Damage at "=" Found, Then Removed by a Gate The first test of side effects (six English sentences) contained no =, and the write-out acts only at =, so it could not fail. A larger test (200 prose passages, 200 Python chunks with 958 = positions, maths lines with 277) found damage at =: in code the bracket circuit raised loss 1.84 → 13.35. A logistic gate on Qwen's last hidden state (1,537 numbers) removes it: every change is 0.00 except +0.02 for ascending digits in code, and task accuracy stays 100%. LoRA raises prose loss by 0.03–0.12.
What Training Did Not Find, and the Limits The trained circuit has the written circuit's architecture, wiring and data, so a solution exists in that space; gradient descent found it on no seed. Even with its learning rate chosen on the length-128 test itself, LoRA reaches only 79.9% on brackets. Of 17 circuits written by the Exp 03 writer model (simple synthetic-token tasks), 14 reach 100% at length 64 once grafted. Limit: the circuit does the computing; Qwen does not learn the skill.

64-Digit Sums 100% Exact · Layer-9 Result Used ≈ Told in Words

Bottom line: With a second written circuit (2 layers, 3 heads, 1,712 non-zero weights), frozen Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct writes the whole sum of Add: 4821+3957= digit by digit (greedy over the full vocabulary) and is exactly right in 100% of cases at 16, 32 and 64 digits and on 64-digit carry chains, on all 3 seeds; a trained circuit with the same inputs and LoRA trained on whole sums score 0% at 32 and 64 digits. Pillar II · Supporting Evidence (Exp 02 & Exp 03)

Exact Verifiers Bootstrap Genuine Weight Compilers, Yet Plateau Without Algorithmic Exploration

When target circuits are tiny (Exp 02, Boolean MLPs), random guessing easily beats LLM self-training. When the target becomes a sparse 2-layer Transformer over variable-length sequences (Exp 03), random search collapses to 4.5%—and a verifier-bootstrapped 3B model invents exact OOD circuits that beat 4,000-step SGD.

Experiment 02 · 02-weight-writer

Weight Writer (Boolean MLPs): Self-Training Works, Random Search Beats It, Nothing Generalizes

Verifier Works · Random Wins (85.4% vs 42.0%)

Experiment 03 · 03-transformer-writer

Pillar III · Supporting Evidence (Exp 05 & Exp 06)

Orthogonal Subspaces & Recurrent Inductive Priors Win Early, Then Meet Geometric Limits

Both continual context-to-weights distillation (Exp 05) and modernized RNNs vs. MicroGPT (Exp 06) confirm the same structural law: clever linear geometry and local inductive bias deliver dramatic early gains, before nonlinear cross-layer interference and fixed-state capacity set hard ceilings.

Experiment 05 · 05-context-to-weights

Zero-Label Context-to-Weights: Orthogonal Rank-2 Slots Beat Shared LoRA 4.3×, Still Leak Downstream

26.6% vs 6.2% Retention · Nonlinear Interference

Bottom line: Sequentially absorbing 16 unseen fictional dossiers (64 atomic facts) from Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct's context window into its weights with zero external labels reaches 100% on Card 1 (0% no-context floor), then collapses to 6.2% (1/16) under both shared SFT LoRA and shared KL LoRA. Constraining each dossier's rank-2 input projection via Gram-Schmidt orthogonalization (A_k ⊥ span(A_1..A_{k-1})) and merging all 16 into a single weight matrix (no router) lifts retention 4.3× to 26.6%—while revealing why input-space orthogonality alone cannot stop downstream interference.

Experiment 06 · 06-domingos-rnn-vs-gpt

Testing Pedro Domingos's Claim: Modernized LSTM/RNN vs. MicroGPT on Common Crawl

LSTM Leads at 65.5M Tok · Both GPTs Lead at 250M Tok

Bottom line: Testing Pedro Domingos's specification ("An RNN with softmax layer, residual stream, layer norm and positional encoding does the same") on Common Crawl (fineweb-edu) with matched parameters (10.65M non-embed / 29.95M total, seq_len=512) in two separate runs. At 65.5M tokens (batch 32, 4,000 steps) domingos_lstm leads microgpt (125.38 vs 170.92 perplexity, also at equal wall-clock) and domingos_rnn leads too (158.71). At 250M tokens (batch 64, 7,630 steps) both GPT variants lead both recurrent models: microgpt_rope 58.16, microgpt 66.84, domingos_lstm 79.43, domingos_rnn 96.96. The recurrent models lead early with little data; the Transformers overtake with more. "Does the same" is not supported at 250M tokens in this small setup. The runs differ in batch size and step count, so they are two runs, not one curve; one seed each.
